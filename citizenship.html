Overview

What Is British Citizenship by Naturalisation?

Naturalisation is the process by which a foreign national becomes a British citizen. It is the final step in most people's UK immigration journey — granting you the full rights of a British citizen, including the right to a British passport, unrestricted right to live and work in the UK, and the ability to pass citizenship to your children.

To naturalise, you must typically have held Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR) or Settled Status for at least 12 months, have lived in the UK for at least 5 years (3 years if you're the spouse or civil partner of a British citizen), and meet the good character requirement.

Requirements

Eligibility Criteria for Naturalisation

ILR or Settled Status — You must hold ILR (Indefinite Leave to Remain) or EU Settled Status at the time of your application.
Residency Requirement — You must have been lawfully in the UK for the last 5 years (or 3 years if married to/in a civil partnership with a British citizen).
Absences — You must not have spent more than 450 nights outside the UK in the last 5 years (270 nights in the last 3 years for spouses), with no more than 90 nights absent in the final year.
Life in the UK Test — You must pass the Life in the UK test (unless you are exempt due to age or physical/mental condition).
English Language — You must demonstrate sufficient knowledge of English (or Welsh or Scottish Gaelic) — typically B1 CEFR level.
Good Character — You must meet the good character requirement. This includes criminal record considerations, financial history, and immigration compliance.
Intention to Remain — You must intend to continue living in the UK (or enter Crown service, or the service of an international organisation, or employment by a UK company).

Step by Step

The Naturalisation Process

1
Eligibility Assessment

We review your residency history, absences, ILR status, and any other factors that could affect your application — before you apply.

2
Life in the UK Test

We advise on study materials and resources. You must pass the 24-question test (minimum 18 correct) at an approved test centre.

3
Application Preparation

We complete the AN form on your behalf, source supporting documentation, and identify appropriate referees who meet the Home Office criteria.

4
Submission

We submit the application online with all supporting documentation, and pay attention to every detail of the submission process.

5
Citizenship Ceremony

Once approved (typically 6 months), you attend a citizenship ceremony to take the Oath of Allegiance and receive your certificate. We prepare you for what to expect.

Common Questions

Citizenship FAQs

The Home Office aims to process straightforward naturalisation applications within 6 months. Complex cases or those requiring additional checks may take longer. There is no premium service for naturalisation applications — it cannot be expedited.
The UK allows dual nationality, so you generally do not need to renounce your current citizenship when becoming British. However, your country of origin may have rules about dual nationality — you should check this with the relevant embassy or authorities before applying.
You can retake the test as many times as needed, but you must wait 7 days between attempts and pay the £50 fee each time. We advise our clients on the best study materials and approach — most people who prepare properly pass on the first attempt.
Children under 18 who are registered as British citizens at the same time as a parent naturalises, or who were born in the UK and are resident here, may be able to register as British citizens. The rules are complex and depend on the child's circumstances. We can advise on whether your children qualify.
The good character requirement covers criminal convictions, civil penalties, immigration breaches, financial matters (such as unpaid taxes), and deception in previous immigration applications. A criminal conviction doesn't automatically disqualify you — it depends on the offence and how long ago it was. We'll assess your individual circumstances and advise honestly on your position.
