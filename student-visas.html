Student Visa

Specialist guidance for international students applying to study in the UK — from CAS to course completion and the Graduate Route beyond.

Overview

What Is the Student Visa?

The UK Student visa (formerly Tier 4) allows nationals from outside the UK to study at a licensed educational institution in the UK. To be eligible, your institution must be a Home Office-approved student sponsor and must issue you a Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS).

The visa is tied to your course — it is typically granted for the length of your course plus an additional period (up to 1 year for courses over 12 months). You can bring dependants in certain circumstances, and after graduation you may be eligible for the Graduate Route.

Getting the financial evidence right is one of the most common reasons for refusals. We ensure your funds are presented exactly as the Home Office requires, avoiding avoidable rejections.

Requirements

Key Eligibility Criteria

Unconditional Offer — You must hold an unconditional offer of a place on a course from a Home Office-licensed student sponsor institution.
Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS) — Your institution must assign you a CAS number, which you include in your visa application. It is valid for 6 months from the date of assignment.
English Language Ability — You must demonstrate English at B2 CEFR level for degree courses, or B1 for below-degree courses, through an approved Secure English Language Test (SELT).
Financial Evidence — You must show sufficient funds to cover your course fees and living costs. Outside London: £1,023/month; inside London: £1,334/month — held for 28 consecutive days before applying.
Genuine Student Requirement — The Home Office must be satisfied that you genuinely intend to study and that the course is credible for your academic background and career intentions.
ATAS Certificate (if applicable) — Students on certain sensitive courses at postgraduate level must hold an Academic Technology Approval Scheme (ATAS) certificate before applying.

Documents Required

Process

The Application Process

1
Receive Your Unconditional Offer

Accept your place and ensure you have an unconditional offer letter from an institution with a current UKVI student sponsor licence.

2
Obtain Your CAS

Your institution assigns a CAS once tuition fees are settled or a payment plan is agreed. This is your application key — don't apply without it.

3
Gather Documents & Evidence

We provide a tailored checklist and check every document — especially your financial evidence, which is the most common cause of refusals.

4
Submit Online Application

We complete the application on your behalf and ensure all information is consistent and accurate, then you book and attend your biometrics appointment.

5
Decision & Travel

Most Student visa decisions are made within 3 weeks from inside the UK (8 weeks from outside). Priority services can speed this up. Once approved, we advise on entry and course start requirements.

Students at below-degree level are usually restricted to 10 hours per week. You cannot be self-employed or work as a professional sportsperson or entertainer.
You need to show enough to cover your course fees for the first year (unless already paid) plus living costs — £1,334 per month if studying in London, or £1,023 per month outside London, up to a maximum of 9 months. These funds must be held in your account for at least 28 consecutive days before the date you apply. Official financial sponsors (your institution or government scholarships) can sometimes cover this requirement.
Dependants can accompany you if you are a government-sponsored student, or if your course is a postgraduate programme lasting 9 months or more at a higher education provider. Students on undergraduate or shorter courses generally cannot bring dependants. Each dependant must also meet their own financial and eligibility requirements.
The Graduate Route allows you to stay in the UK for 2 years (or 3 years if you completed a PhD) after graduating to work or look for work. You do not need a job offer and there is no salary threshold. It is an unsponsored route, so you can work in almost any job. After completing the Graduate Route, many switch to the Skilled Worker route to remain long-term.
