Overview

What Is ILR (Indefinite Leave to Remain)?

Indefinite Leave to Remain — commonly known as ILR or settlement — is a form of immigration permission that allows you to live and work in the UK permanently with no time restrictions on your stay.

Unlike a standard visa, ILR has no expiry date. Once granted, you can live and work in the UK without immigration restrictions, access public funds, and use the NHS without paying the Immigration Health Surcharge. After a qualifying period, ILR holders can apply for British citizenship.

There are multiple routes to ILR depending on your immigration history — skilled worker route, family route, long residence, and others. The right pathway depends entirely on your individual circumstances.

Requirements

General ILR Eligibility Requirements

While specific requirements vary by route, these criteria apply to most ILR applications:

Qualifying Residence Period — You must have spent the required number of years in the UK on a qualifying visa (typically 5 years for work/family routes, 10 years for long residence).
Continuous Lawful Residence — Your stay must have been lawful and continuous. You must not have broken your conditions of leave or been absent from the UK for more than 180 days in any 12-month period.
No Unspent Convictions — You must not have any unspent criminal convictions, and you must meet the good character requirement where it applies to your route.
Life in the UK Test — You must pass the Life in the UK test (unless exempt due to age under 18 or over 65, or physical or mental disability).
English Language — Depending on your route, you must demonstrate English language ability at B1 CEFR level or above.
Current Leave — Your current leave must not have expired, and the application must usually be submitted before your current visa expires.

Why It Matters

Don't Risk Your ILR Application

An ILR refusal is serious. It doesn't just mean you have to reapply — it can affect your ability to obtain British citizenship in the future and may create complications for dependent family members.

Common reasons for ILR refusals include miscalculated absences, overlooked criminal convictions, incomplete documentation, and failure to meet the continuous residence requirement. Our consultants check every aspect of your application before submission.

We've helped clients with complex immigration histories, extended absences, and previous refusals successfully obtain ILR. Whatever your history, we'll give you an honest assessment of your position.

Common Questions

ILR FAQs

For most routes, you must not have been absent from the UK for more than 180 days in any rolling 12-month period during the qualifying period. The 10-year long residence route has stricter requirements. We carefully calculate your absences — even a borderline case can be problematic, so accurate counting is essential.
ILR itself doesn't have an expiry date. However, if you spend more than 2 years continuously outside the UK, your ILR lapses and you lose your settled status. If you plan to spend extended periods abroad, we can advise on how to protect your settlement status or whether a different arrangement is appropriate.
You can submit your ILR application up to 28 days before your 5-year qualifying period is complete. We advise clients on the exact application date to ensure timing is correct and the application window is used optimally.
If you have lived in the UK lawfully for 10 continuous years, you may be eligible for ILR under the long residence route — even if you've been on a mix of different visas. This is a complex route with strict continuous residence requirements. We regularly handle long residence ILR applications, including cases with complicated immigration histories.
Once you have ILR, you can live and work in the UK permanently without immigration restrictions. After holding ILR for 12 months (or as a spouse of a British citizen — immediately), you become eligible to apply for British citizenship by naturalisation. We'll advise you on the naturalisation process at this stage.
