Planning & Design FAQs

We answer these questions every week. If your question isn't here, call us on 01282 450085 or send an enquiry and we'll get back to you within one working day.

It depends on the size and type of extension. Many single-storey rear extensions fall under Permitted Development (PD) rights, which means you can build without submitting a full planning application. However, PD rights have strict limits on size, height, materials and proximity to boundaries.

Generally, a single-storey rear extension can extend up to 3 metres from the original rear wall for terraced or semi-detached homes, or 4 metres for detached properties, without planning permission. Double-storey extensions, side extensions and any work in conservation areas or on listed buildings almost always require planning permission.

The safest approach is to discuss your specific project with us first. We'll tell you quickly whether PD applies or whether a planning application is needed, and we can also apply for a Lawful Development Certificate if you want written confirmation from the council that your project is permitted development.

Permitted Development (PD) is a set of national planning rules that allow certain types of work to be carried out without a planning application. The rules cover extensions, loft conversions, garage conversions, garden buildings, porches and more.

PD rights can be removed or restricted in certain situations, including: conservation areas, Article 4 Direction areas (where the council has removed PD rights locally), listed buildings, flats and maisonettes, and some new-build properties where the original developer removed PD rights as part of the planning consent.

When you contact us, we'll check whether your property has any PD restrictions and advise you on the best route forward. If PD applies, we can still prepare drawings and apply for a Lawful Development Certificate so you have written proof for mortgage lenders and future buyers.

The council has a statutory 8-week target to decide on a householder planning application (the type used for most extensions and home alterations). Larger or more complex applications, including new builds and commercial projects, have a 13-week determination period.

In practice, decisions sometimes take longer. Councils can request additional information during the process, which pauses the clock, and some authorities are busier than others. Based on our experience with East Lancashire's planning authorities, most straightforward householder applications are decided within 8 to 12 weeks of submission.

Before you can submit, we need time to complete the design drawings — typically 2 to 4 weeks depending on the complexity of the project. So from our first meeting to a planning decision, you should allow roughly 12 to 16 weeks for a standard residential application.

Planning permission deals with the principle of development — whether you're allowed to build something at all, taking into account the impact on neighbours, the street scene and the character of the area. It's granted by the Local Planning Authority (your local council).

Building regulations are a separate set of technical standards that ensure your building is structurally safe, energy efficient and compliant with fire safety, drainage and accessibility requirements. They're approved by a Building Control Body — either through the council or a private approved inspector.

Most building projects need both. You typically get planning permission first to establish that you can build, then building regulations approval to confirm how you build it. Some projects — such as garage conversions and internal alterations — don't need planning permission but do need building regulations. We handle both as part of our full service.

Many loft conversions do fall within Permitted Development, provided they meet certain criteria. A loft conversion that adds less than 40 cubic metres of additional roof space on a terraced house (50 cubic metres for detached and semi-detached) and doesn't extend higher than the highest part of the existing roof typically doesn't require planning permission.

However, there are restrictions. Dormer windows on the front elevation visible from the road almost always require planning permission. Properties in conservation areas cannot add roof alterations under PD. And if the roof space has already been previously enlarged, the permitted allowance may already be used up.

All loft conversions — regardless of whether they need planning permission — do require building regulations approval, covering structural changes, fire escape routes, staircase design and insulation. We'll guide you through exactly what's needed for your specific property and location.

Our fees depend on the size and complexity of your project. We offer fixed-fee quotes rather than percentage-based fees, so you'll know exactly what you're paying before any work begins.

As a rough guide, architectural fees for a straightforward planning application (drawings only, no building regulations) typically start from around £600 to £900 for a single-storey extension. A full service including planning application, design revisions and building regulations drawings for a double-storey extension or loft conversion typically ranges from £1,200 to £2,500 depending on scope.

New build projects are priced individually based on the scale of the build. We always provide a detailed written quote after the initial consultation, so you can make an informed decision before committing to anything. The initial consultation is always free.

A planning refusal isn't necessarily the end of the road. If your application is refused, the council will provide a decision notice explaining the specific reasons for refusal.

In many cases, the design can be amended to address the council's concerns and resubmitted free of charge (within 12 months of the original refusal on a householder application). This is often the quickest route to approval. We'll review the reasons for refusal and advise you on whether an amended resubmission is likely to succeed.

Alternatively, you have the right to appeal to the Planning Inspectorate. Appeals are free to submit but take time — typically 6 to 12 months for a written representations appeal. We can advise you on whether an appeal is likely to be successful and support you through the process. Before submitting any application, we always carry out a pre-application assessment to minimise the risk of refusal.

The timeline varies by project type, but for a typical residential extension or loft conversion, here's a realistic guide:

Weeks 1–2: Free consultation, site survey and brief agreed.
Weeks 3–5: Design drawings produced, reviewed and approved by you.
Weeks 5–13: Planning application submitted and determined (8-week statutory period).
Weeks 13–16: Building regulations drawings prepared and submitted.
Week 16+: You engage a contractor and work begins on site.

So from first contact to starting on site, allow roughly 4 to 6 months for a project that requires planning permission. Projects under permitted development (no planning needed) can move significantly faster — often on site within 8 to 12 weeks of our first meeting.

The title "architect" is legally protected in the UK and only individuals registered with the Architects Registration Board (ARB) can use it. However, you don't legally need an ARB-registered architect to prepare and submit a planning application — a skilled draughtsperson or architectural technologist can do this too.

At Peak Architecture Ltd, Haseeb Aslam holds architectural qualifications and brings full design expertise to every project. What matters most is the quality of the design, the accuracy of the drawings and the knowledge of local planning policy — not the title alone.
