Refusal & Appeals

Challenging a Refusal

A refusal is not always the end. Depending on the type of decision, you may be able to request an Administrative Review, submit a Reconsideration, or lodge a formal Appeal before an independent tribunal.

Overview

Your Options After a Refusal

Receiving a refusal letter from the Home Office can feel devastating, but it does not necessarily mean your case is over. UK immigration law provides several mechanisms for challenging a decision, and the right option depends on the type of application, the reasons given for refusal, and the timeframes involved.

It is critical to act quickly. Strict time limits apply to all challenge routes — missing a deadline can permanently close an option that would otherwise have been available to you.

Our IAA regulated lawyers analyse every refusal in detail, identify the strongest available route, and act without delay to protect your position.

Discuss Your Refusal

The Three Main Routes

AR
Administrative Review A review by the Home Office of an eligible casework decision. Challenges caseworker errors.
RC
Reconsideration Asking the Home Office to look again, typically where new evidence or a change of circumstance applies.
AP
Tribunal Appeal An independent hearing before the First-tier Tribunal (Immigration & Asylum Chamber).
Route 1

Administrative Review

An Administrative Review (AR) is available where the Home Office has made a casework error in refusing or cancelling your permission to enter or remain in the UK. It is not a full reconsideration of your application — it asks a different Home Office caseworker to check whether a specific error was made.

Common grounds for an AR include: the caseworker applied the wrong Immigration Rules, incorrectly assessed your points or evidence, failed to consider relevant material, or made a procedural mistake.

Who Can Apply — Those refused leave to remain inside the UK, or those refused entry clearance or leave to enter in certain categories including PBS routes.
Time Limit — You must apply within 14 days of receiving the refusal decision if you are in the UK; 28 days if you are outside the UK.
Fee — There is a Home Office fee payable with an AR application. We will confirm the current fee when advising you.
Outcome — If the AR is successful, the original decision is withdrawn and a fresh decision is made on your application. If unsuccessful, you may still have other options.

AR is NOT available for all refusals

Administrative Review is only available for certain types of decisions. It is not available where you have a right of appeal to the Tribunal, or for decisions made outside the scope of the Immigration Rules (such as some human rights refusals).

Before submitting, we identify all available grounds and assess whether an AR is the right route — or whether a full appeal or reconsideration would offer stronger prospects.

Route 2

Reconsideration

When Is Reconsideration the Right Route?

A Reconsideration is a fresh request to the Home Office to look again at a decision. This is typically used where circumstances have changed since the original decision, where significant new evidence has come to light, or where an AR is not available for the decision type.

It is also used in cases where there has been no caseworker error as such, but the overall decision was wrong — for example, where discretion was exercised incorrectly or relevant guidance was not followed.

Reconsiderations are not a formally defined route in the same way as Administrative Reviews or appeals — instead, they involve making representations to the Home Office setting out why the decision should not stand.

This might take the form of a pre-action letter before judicial review, a formal complaint, or a fresh application with a covering letter where the Home Office has misdirected itself in law. The appropriate approach depends on the case.

New Evidence — Where material evidence was not submitted first time or was submitted but overlooked.
Change of Circumstances — Where your situation has materially changed since the original decision.
Incorrect Application of Guidance — Where the caseworker failed to follow published Home Office guidance relevant to your case.
Compassionate Factors — In certain categories, compelling compassionate circumstances can be raised in representations to the Home Office.
Route 3

Immigration Appeals

Where you have a right of appeal, your case is heard by an independent judge at the First-tier Tribunal (Immigration and Asylum Chamber). The Tribunal is completely separate from the Home Office and will review the legality and correctness of the decision.

Appeals are generally available where a refusal engages your human rights (for example, refusals of spouse/partner visas or where you have a private or family life in the UK), or where the Home Office has revoked or cancelled existing leave in certain circumstances.

The hearing gives you the opportunity to present your case before a judge, call witnesses, and challenge the Home Office's reasoning directly. The Tribunal can allow the appeal outright, allow it with directions, or dismiss it.

Time Limit — If you are in the UK, you must lodge your appeal within 14 days of receiving the refusal. If you are abroad, the deadline is 28 days. These time limits are strictly enforced.
Grounds of Appeal — An appeal can be made on the grounds that the decision is unlawful under the Human Rights Act 1998, or under the Immigration Rules, or on other grounds where permitted by statute.
Paper or Oral Hearing — Some appeals are decided on the papers alone; others proceed to a full oral hearing. We prepare both the written bundle and advocate your case at the hearing.
Upper Tribunal — If the First-tier Tribunal dismisses your appeal, there may be grounds to seek permission to appeal further to the Upper Tribunal on a point of law.

Do I Have a Right of Appeal?

Spouse, partner or family visa refusals (human rights grounds)
Asylum claims and protection decisions
Deportation and removal decisions
EEA decisions (certain transitional cases)
Deprivation of citizenship decisions

Many refusals — including most PBS route refusals — do not carry a right of appeal and must be challenged through AR or fresh application. We advise you clearly from the outset.

Why Act Fast

Time Limits Are Critical

In refusal cases, delays can be fatal to your options. Administrative Review deadlines of 14 days pass quickly, and Appeal Notices must be lodged within strict statutory timeframes. Out-of-time applications are generally not accepted.

If you have received a refusal — whether you were expecting it or not — contact us immediately. Even if you have only a few days remaining, we can act quickly to protect your position.
